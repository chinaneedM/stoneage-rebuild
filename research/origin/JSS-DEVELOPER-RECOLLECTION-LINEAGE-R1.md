# JSS Developer Recollection Lineage — R1

Date: 2026-09-18

Purpose: preserve and classify a newly recovered chain of later mirrors/transcriptions that appear to preserve first-person recollections from a person directly involved in the original JSS `STONEAGE` project.

This material is potentially extremely valuable for origin archaeology, but its authorship and original publication dates are not yet fully closed. It therefore remains separate from contemporaneous 1999 FACT evidence.

## 1. Evidence classes used here

- **A** — contemporaneous first-party/press material. None of the recollection mirrors qualify merely because they preserve first-person text.
- **B candidate** — later first-party developer recollection whose author identity/provenance has been independently verified.
- **C / preserved recollection lead** — later mirror, translation, transcription, or community preservation whose original author/source is plausible but not yet independently authenticated.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — inference connecting the preserved first-person author to a named JSS staff member.

No claim in this file should be promoted to 1999 FACT solely because the remembered event concerns 1998–1999.

## 3. Recollection #2 — original working title `Jomon`

Mirror:

https://pooyas.com/index.php?document_srl=157216&mid=sa_histoy

### Preserved claims

The first-person author says:

- the earliest working/development name was `Jomon`;
- another planner had prepared the initial proposal before the author directly took charge;
- the author worried that the name would constrain the project too strongly to the historical Jōmon period;
- because Tokyo Game Show participation had already been decided, a replacement title had to be selected quickly;
- brainstormed candidates included `StoneAge`, `はしれ原始人`, `原始時代`, `ぼくら原始人`, and `StoneLife`;
- `StoneAge` was ultimately chosen as memorable, easy to say, and easy to visualize;
- the author also casually recalls that the same group may have named `CrossGate`.

### Evidence significance

If authorship is authenticated, this would be direct developer recollection evidence that the original concept predated the final `StoneAge` title and that the project intentionally avoided being confined to a literal reconstruction of Japan's Jōmon period.

For now these remain **C-level recollection claims**, not contemporaneous 1999 FACT.

## 4. Recollection #1 — project inheritance and core concepts

A 2025 Korean preservation post reproduces/translates a chapter attributed to the `StoneAge Memories` site and cites the same original domain:

https://gall.dcinside.com/mgallery/board/view/?id=stonepc&no=2237

### Preserved claims

The first-person narrative says, in substance:

- the underlying proposal existed before the author became responsible for it;
- the author had been occupied with completion of `Chameleon Twist 2` during late 1998;
- the previous StoneAge planner left around the end of December;
- the author was told around the New Year work-start period that the project would be handed over to them;
- a meeting with NTT Data followed immediately;
- the project was under an extremely short development schedule, described as roughly six months;
- the early proposal was only a few pages and centered on three broad concepts: a Stone Age motif, a world that grows/changes, and living together with dinosaurs;
- the author retained the Stone Age motif and dinosaur coexistence concepts but considered the dynamically growing-world concept impractical under the schedule;
- the author's own specification emphasis became: a distinctive prehistoric world, communication-oriented online RPG play, and life/coexistence with dinosaurs;
- communication features such as cards/name-card style exchange and character actions were conceived as ways to move beyond text chat;
- pet recruitment, naming and leveling were implemented, while a fuller simulation of living with dinosaurs remained unrealized.

### Evidence significance

This recollection closely matches the independently documented May 1999 public design language about community and a broader life-oriented world, while also explaining which ambitions may have been reduced under schedule pressure.

The correspondence increases plausibility but does not itself authenticate the author.

## 5. Recollection #5 — NTT Data / Gamer's Dream coordination

Mirror:

https://pooyas.com/index.php?document_srl=157244&mid=sa_histoy

The page preserves both Korean translation and Japanese source text.

### Preserved claims

The first-person author describes:

- traveling from the Osaka-side development company to Tokyo for Tokyo Game Show coordination;
- visiting the NTT Data building in Toyosu;
- contacting the Gamer's Dream representative there;
- attending a meeting with other Gamer's Dream titles/companies, including Dark Eyes / Nextech and Blue Sphere / Banpresto participants;
- considering the trip a privilege of being a director.

### Correlation with contemporaneous evidence

Independent 1999 PC Watch reporting places `STONEAGE` in NTT Data's Gamer's Dream booth at Tokyo Game Show. The recollection therefore describes a relationship independently attested by contemporaneous press.

This correlation materially strengthens the recollection's plausibility, but does not establish the author's name by itself.

## 6. Character-development recollection

Pooya mirror:

https://pooyas.com/index.php?document_srl=157268&mid=sa_histoy

### Preserved claims

The first-person author says:

- some project planning and a village image illustration existed before the author accepted/was told of the project-leader role;
- the broad visual direction was retained;
- designers were asked for a diverse cast appealing to male and female players;
- roughly 20 character concepts were produced and 12 were used;
- schedule pressure prevented all concepts from being implemented;
- the author personally directed many character-motion ideas together with the 3D animator;
- character motions were intentionally designed for personality and comic individuality;
- the author expresses satisfaction with the final character identity and says they would like to use the same designer if a StoneAge 2 were ever made.

### Research value

This is potentially useful for distinguishing original character/world design intent from later localization or sequel-era additions. The mirror also preserves images labelled as early/unselected character concepts, but those images have not yet been provenance-audited in this project.

## 7. Early Chinese secondary witness — 2002

Source:

https://news.17173.com/z/stoneage/content/2002-5-17/n82_998940.html

Published: 2002-05-17

Author: 阿花

### Why this matters

The author explicitly says that the earliest Japanese-development section was based on articles written by developers on a Japanese website, while warning that their own Japanese ability was weak and that the piece mixed history with personal recollection.

This article therefore acts as an **early witness that developer articles were publicly accessible by 2002**, substantially earlier than the 2024/2025 Korean mirrors.

It repeats a name rendered in Chinese as `森冈训`, describes early-1999 project leadership, title brainstorming, 256-color decisions, a development team of a little over 20 people, and the September beta.

### Classification

**C / early secondary witness.**

Important limitation: `森冈训` must not be treated as a verified Japanese personal name. It may be a mistranslation, transcription error, partial name, nickname, or unrelated rendering. The 2002 author explicitly warns of weak Japanese comprehension.

## 8. Identity hypothesis — possible connection to Hiroyuki Morioka

Independent JSS game credits provide a strong but not yet conclusive identity clue.

### Independent credit evidence

The Japanese N64 release of `Chameleon Twist 2`, dated 1998-12-25 and credited to Japan System Supply, lists:

- **Director: Hiroyuki Morioka**
- CG Design: Hiroyuki Morioka
- Stage Design: Hiroyuki Morioka

Preserved staff-credit transcription:

https://raido.moe/staff/n64/n64_chameleon_twist_2.html

MobyGames independently reports the same English-credit name and role for the same JSS title.

### Why the match is significant

The StoneAge recollection author says they were heavily occupied with `Chameleon Twist 2` near its completion and then took over the StoneAge project immediately after the previous planner departed. Another preserved recollection describes Tokyo Game Show/NTT Data coordination as a director's privilege.

A JSS employee named **Hiroyuki Morioka** being the documented director of the exact game named in the recollection is therefore a strong biographical match.

### Current classification

**HYPOTHESIS — strong, unresolved identity match.**

Do not yet write:

`StoneAge project leader = Hiroyuki Morioka`

as FACT.

### What is still required to close the identity

At least one of:

1. original `sa.sc-36.net` page with author/profile/byline;
2. archived site index/about page naming the writer;
3. original Japanese StoneAge credits/manual/interview naming Hiroyuki Morioka as project leader/director/planner;
4. another independent first-party or contemporaneous source explicitly connecting Hiroyuki Morioka to StoneAge;
5. verified Japanese spelling that explains or disproves the old Chinese rendering `森冈训`.

## 9. New origin-model implications — kept provisional

The recollection lineage suggests a more specific origin model worth testing against primary artifacts:

### HYPOTHESIS A — inherited concept, then redirected

StoneAge may have begun as a small earlier proposal provisionally called `Jomon`, with a stronger concept of a world that develops/grows. A later project leader may have inherited it around the turn of 1998/1999 and refocused it toward a broader original prehistoric world plus communication RPG and dinosaur coexistence.

### HYPOTHESIS B — original world deliberately not literal prehistory

The title change away from `Jomon` and the recalled desire for a distinctive prehistoric world fit the project's existing hypothesis that the earliest StoneAge world was intentionally playful/original rather than a strict archaeological simulation.

### HYPOTHESIS C — communication was structural, not incidental

The recollection's emphasis on communication mechanics aligns with contemporaneous public descriptions of community/cooperation. This suggests social interaction may have been a core original design pillar rather than merely an MMO necessity.

These hypotheses should now guide searches for contemporaneous specifications, screenshots and manuals, but they remain subordinate to primary evidence.
