# Source Registry Supplement — JSS Developer Recollections R1

Date: 2026-09-18

Purpose: structured source records for the newly recovered StoneAge developer-recollection lineage. This supplement exists because these sources have a fundamentally different evidentiary character from contemporaneous 1999 press, archived official JSS pages, and physical artifacts.

The canonical research analysis is `research/origin/JSS-DEVELOPER-RECOLLECTION-LINEAGE-R1.md`.

## Evidence rule

- A later mirror preserving first-person Japanese text is **not** automatically a first-party source.
- A developer recollection can become **B** only when authorship/provenance is independently authenticated.
- Until then, mirror/transcription/translation evidence remains **C**, even when its internal details correlate strongly with contemporaneous facts.
- Independent game credits can prove a person's role on the credited game, but not their StoneAge role unless StoneAge is explicitly named.

## SRC-JP-SA-MEMORIES-POOYA-TITLE-02

- Title: `#2 게임 타이틀 정하기` / preserved Japanese original `○その２当初の企画名は・・・`
- Retrieval date: 2026-09-18
- Source type: later Korean mirror preserving substantial Japanese original text
- URL: https://pooyas.com/index.php?document_srl=157216&mid=sa_histoy
- Original-source attribution printed by mirror: `http://www.sa.sc-36.net/`
- Confidence: **C**, candidate for **B** only if original author identity is authenticated
- Preserved recollection claims:
  - initial development/working title was `Jomon`;
  - an earlier planner prepared the initial proposal before the narrator directly took responsibility;
  - the narrator wanted to avoid over-constraining the game to the literal Jōmon period;
  - Tokyo Game Show participation was already decided, creating urgency around the final title;
  - brainstormed names included `StoneAge`, `はしれ原始人`, `原始時代`, `ぼくら原始人`, and `StoneLife`;
  - `StoneAge` was selected as easy to remember/say/visualize;
  - the narrator casually recalls that the same group may also have named `CrossGate`.
- Does not establish:
  - the narrator's identity;
  - a contemporaneous 1998/1999 document using the name `Jomon`;
  - exact chronology beyond the narrator's later memory.

- Original date: 2002-05-17
- Author: 阿花

- Language/region: Chinese / Mainland China

- Confidence: **C**

- Article claims / leads:
  - renders a project-leader name as `森冈训`;
  - repeats early-1999 project-lead/title-development material;

- Research value:
  - acts as an early witness that the developer-recollection corpus existed years before the modern Korean mirrors.

## Identity hypothesis controlled by this source group

**HYPOTHESIS — strong, unresolved:** the narrator of the preserved StoneAge recollections may be Hiroyuki Morioka.

Reasons the hypothesis is worth testing:

1. narrator says they were occupied with `Chameleon Twist 2` near its completion in late 1998;
2. narrator says they took over StoneAge immediately after the previous planner left around year-end;
3. narrator describes TGS/NTT coordination as a director-related privilege;
4. independent Japanese N64 credits place Hiroyuki Morioka as Director of `Chameleon Twist 2`, released 1998-12-25;
5. earlier `Chameleon Twist` credits already place Morioka in Game Design / Computer Arts.

Reasons it remains unresolved:

1. no recovered `sa.sc-36.net` author/profile/byline currently names Hiroyuki Morioka;
2. no recovered original StoneAge credit/manual/interview currently names him as project leader/director;
3. the 2002 Chinese rendering `森冈训` is not a reliable Japanese-name identification.

Promotion criterion: require an original-site byline/profile, original StoneAge staff credit, contemporaneous interview, or equivalent independent evidence explicitly linking Hiroyuki Morioka to StoneAge.
